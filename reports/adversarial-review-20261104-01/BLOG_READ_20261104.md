4. p* versus model size, and epochs
- Shape: "Within a given model family and number of epochs, … often increases at small scales (particularly through tens of billions of parameters) and decreases at larger scales as we near the hundreds of billions of parameters regime (for reference, the largest pictured JS-dense rung has fewer than 100B parameters)."

6. Proposed explanation
Yes, offered explicitly as a hypothesis: "We hypothesize that this is because models learn different patterns present in the data at different rates." Also: "Some patterns generalize well across all sequences (for example, understanding English), while others are idiosyncratic to smaller groups of sequences." And "We therefore hypothesize that smaller models use their limited capacity to learn general patterns and cannot represent idiosyncratic patterns, medium-sized models learn both the general and the most important idiosyncratic patterns (by sequence weight), and large models have the capacity to learn all idiosyncratic patterns with all weights." Finally: "Epoching allows the model to learn more of both the general and idiosyncratic patterns." The medium-model claim is more specific than "add sequence-specific patterns": it covers the most important idiosyncratic patterns, ranked by sequence weight.
