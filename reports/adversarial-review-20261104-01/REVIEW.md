## 1. Verdict

A skeptical referee would accept the operational discipline (hash-sealed protocols, labels fixed before outcomes, recomputed reports, preserved failures) and four narrow facts: on a synthetic toy, the p\*-peak width moves smaller between E15 and E40 (S); on WikiText-2 after 32 epochs at 3e-5, p\* over 384–768 falls at E32 and each width's p\* peaks earlier in epochs the wider the model (N4); on WikiText-103 at 3 epochs, p\* at 768 is below p\* at 512 at matched training progress at both rates tested (N6, reanalysed here); and p\* at a fixed update count depends on the learning rate differently at 512 and at 768 (N6). The referee would reject the framing of all five claims as tests of Jane Street's findings: every model here is at or below Jane Street's smallest rung ("tens of millions" of parameters; blog read, item 4), is trained on 0.2–2.1 M unique tokens (0.18–0.61 adaptation token-passes per parameter; recompute: `tokens`), and the "capacity" whose peak is being located is the capacity to memorize a tiny dataset, in regimes Jane Street avoided by regularizing to maximize held-out performance (blog read, item 5). Within that reduced scope, the referee would further reject three labels as stated: CAPACITY_BEYOND_MEMORIZATION (M rejects a straw man; the project's own fixtures give the same label to a curve with no width effect at all), RATE_DEPENDENT (a fixed-update artifact: at matched training progress the 1e-4 ordering is 512 above 768 in 6 of 6 draws, and the sealed interval loses significance when either of two draws is left out), and INTERIOR_PEAK as a rise-and-fall (the 384→512 rise mostly vanishes at matched progress; the 512→768 fall survives). Pre-registration is local hash-sealing verified by the same two actors, public for one study only and after its data existed; two of five natural-text runs were second attempts on the same seeds. The paper this could support is a small-scale methodological study of p\* under training-progress and learning-rate confounds, not a replication of Jane Street.

## 2. The five claims

### Claim 1. "On synthetic data and on WikiText-2 (overfit regime), more training moves the p\* peak to smaller models, as Jane Street found (pre-registered, fresh data)."

**Strongest counter-argument.** The sign matches Jane Street's sentence, but neither study measures Jane Street's phenomenon, and the natural-text "shift" is one fact counted twice.

- **Synthetic (S): the peak is located on curves at the estimator's ceiling, on a task built to memorize.** 19 of the 59 defined primary points have their largest fit at the bound p = 8, and in 10 of them two or more widths tie at 8 (recompute: `S_saturation`; S REPORT §5: 15 fits at the bound at E15, 11 at E20). The location rule then operates on clipped values and tie-breaks ("ties to the smaller width", S PROTOCOL §4). p\* on this task ranges 0.02–8.0 with median 1.76 (recompute: `S_saturation`), against 0.02–0.56 on text: the task is a different regime of the estimator. The task is the project's own generator with 16 group rules and random per-sequence "instance" answers (`vendor/core.py` lines 56–83), 512 training rows and 40 epochs (S PROTOCOL §2); the direction and the epoch window were chosen after seeing them on the exposed ladder (S PROTOCOL §1, §9: "a fresh-corpus confirmation of a pattern already seen, not an independent discovery"). The S32 and SD sensitivities agree (S REPORT §2), so the direction is real on this toy; what it is evidence of is unclear, since the cause "is not identified" (S REPORT §8) and the same shift appears without within-batch weight heterogeneity (S_batch, S REPORT §3).
- **Natural text (N4): P3 is nested in P1.** L(E8) is the top rung, log2 768 = 9.585, in 4 of 4 draws, so S = 9.585 − L(E32) and S > 0 exactly when the E32 peak lies below 768 (N4 RESULT §2 per-draw table; recompute: `N4_P1_P3_per_draw`). P1 TURNS_DOWN (p\* falling from 384 to 768 at E32) all but guarantees that. The chain lists P1 and P3 as two confirmations (chain §2 table); they are one. The interval "+0.909 [0.864, 0.954] doublings" is an interval for 9.585 − L(E32), a censored quantity whose other end is a constant; the protocol itself calls S a bound under an untestable condition (N4 PROTOCOL §6, "When S is a lower bound"; N4 RESULT §2b).
- **The regime is the one Jane Street excluded.** P1 and P3 are read 28 and 24 epochs after the validation minimum of 384–768 (N4 RESULT §4d: lowest validation NLL at E4), with train NLL 2.54 at 768 against validation 6.45 (N4 RESULT §4b/4d; recompute: `N4_by_epoch.E32`). Jane Street: "We apply heavy regularization in all cases such that the resulting model approximately maximizes performance on a held-out set" and "We train for 3 epochs" (blog read, items 5; `docs/references/jane-street-blog-excerpts.md`). The sealed reading says this ("a heavily overfit regime, unlike Jane Street's 3-epoch setting"; N4 RESULT §2a), but the claim as phrased drops it.
- **A plainer description is available and points to memorization saturation.** Per width, p\* rises and then falls with epochs, and the fall starts earlier the wider the model: panel p\* peaks at E16 for 768 (3 of 4 draws; E24 in f02), E24 for 512 (4 of 4), E32 for 384 and 256 (recompute: `N4_epoch_of_peak_p_by_width`; N3 on ten draws: 768 at E16 or E24, 512 at E24 in 8 of 10, every smaller width at E32). "The peak moves smaller" is a restatement of "the widest model's p\* declines first", and the decline coincides with the widest model memorizing its 196,608-token draw (train NLL 2.54, gap 3.9 at E32). At E32 the share of training sequences with negative gain is 13% at 384 and 512 but 6% at 768 (recompute: `N4_negative_gain_fraction_E32_by_width`; N3: 15%, 14%, 7%): the widest model has memorized even the sequences the narrower models degraded, which is what flattens a weight-dependent gain profile. This is consistent with Jane Street's hypothesis for *large* models ("the capacity to learn all idiosyncratic patterns with all weights", blog read, item 6), reached here at 35 M parameters because the dataset is tiny. No per-sequence check was possible under this brief; the point stands as an alternative the project has not excluded.
- **In the few-epoch regime the sign reverses.** N5's secondary reads MOVES_LARGER, +0.0237 [+0.0117, +0.0357] doublings per doubling of readout, 10 of 10 (N5 RESULT §3a). The magnitude is negligible (536 → 560), but the claim's "more training moves the peak smaller" holds only in the overfit regime.

**Severity: MAJOR.** The pre-registered direction result is real; "as Jane Street found" is not supported.

**What would resolve it.** Wording: "the same sign as Jane Street's finding (2), in a synthetic toy in which the estimator saturates, and on WikiText-2 in a 32-epoch overfit regime; not a reproduction". Reanalysis (free): report P3 as implied by P1 given censoring; report the per-width epoch of peak p\* and the E32 negative-gain shares beside P1. New experiment, if the saturation account is to be excluded: a uniform-weight arm at matched steps so that gains are measured against a trained baseline rather than E0 (one N4-sized run with 8 adaptations per draw instead of 4; N4's run took 1.36 h for four draws, N4 RESULT §5, so about 2.5 h), or a dataset-size sweep at fixed width to show whether the epoch of peak p\* tracks tokens per parameter.

### Claim 2. "On WikiText-2 late in training, p\* falls with width past 384 (pre-registered, 4 fresh draws)."

**Strongest counter-argument.** The fall was measured at a single learning rate that was never selected for the widths it is a claim about, in a design where the project itself shows that the rate reorders p\* across widths.

- **3e-5 was imposed on 512 and 768.** N1 selected rates per width from {3e-5, 1e-4, 3e-4} on widths 64–384 only; 128–384 selected 3e-5 *at the bottom edge of the grid*, flagged (N1 REPORT §5: "Edge flags (selected rate at a grid edge): 64, 128, 192, 256, 384"). N3 then ran "64, 96, 128, 192, 256, 384, 512 and 768, at 3e-5 for every width (owner decision 20261030-01, item 3)" (N3 PROTOCOL §2) and N4 kept it (N4 PROTOCOL §3). No validation NLL ever compared rates at 512 or 768 in this regime. Under the project's own claim 5, a width ordering at one rate is uninterpretable as a width effect; the edge flags say the rate may be too high for 128–384 and nothing is known for 512–768.
- **It is a fixed-epoch ordering of models at very different stages.** At E32, training NLL is 4.10, 3.48 and 2.54 at 384, 512 and 768 (recompute: `N4_by_epoch.E32`): the three models are not comparable states, and p\* per width is non-monotone in epochs (claim 1). The protocol concedes "Fixed epochs mix capacity with training progress" (N4 PROTOCOL §11).
- **Regime, as in claim 1.** 20–28 epochs past the validation minimum (N4 RESULT §4d); Jane Street's models were regularized to maximize held-out performance (blog read, item 5).
- **Statistics are not the problem.** The effect is large relative to the draw-to-draw spread (−0.1886 [−0.2083, −0.1688], 4 of 4; N4 RESULT §2) and would survive any reasonable test. The fresh draws share the pre-training pool, validation set and tokenizer with the seen draws, and the tokenizer was trained on every training row including the fresh articles (N4 RESULT §6); this does not threaten P1.

**Severity: MAJOR.** Survives narrowed to its recipe.

**What would resolve it.** Wording: "at 3e-5, a rate selected only for 128–384 and at that grid's edge, 20–28 epochs past the validation minimum". No reanalysis can remove the rate confound: no other rate exists at 32 epochs for 512 or 768. New experiment: 512 and 768 at three rates (1e-4, 3e-5, 1e-5) for 32 epochs on four draws at N = 1,536. N4's stage took about 1,095 s for four widths with pre-training (N4 RESULT §5), so 24 adaptations plus pre-training is roughly 2 h on the local GPU. WikiText-2's training split is exhausted at this N (N4 PROTOCOL §11), so the draws would be WikiText-103 articles, which changes the corpus.

### Claim 3. "On WikiText-103 in 3 epochs without overfitting, a memorization-only account of p\* is rejected (matched-gap test M)."

**Strongest counter-argument.** The account rejected is a straw man, and the test cannot tell a capacity effect from training progress; the project's own fixtures say so, and the label name says the opposite.

- **The account.** "The memorization-only account: p\* is one function of the train–validation gap, the same at every width" (N5 PROTOCOL §1). Nothing in the literature or in the project's earlier studies holds that p\* is a function of the gap alone; N1's exploratory note and N3's TRACKS_MEMORIZATION are rank associations across widths at fixed epochs (N1 exploratory note §3; N3 RESULT §2), not a one-variable law.
- **The fixtures.** "Epoch-only (the confound)": a curve in which p\* depends on the readout only and is equal across widths reads CAPACITY_BEYOND_MEMORIZATION in 96.0–99.2% of replicates (N5 PROTOCOL §9 table; §6b: "A nonzero M therefore also follows if p\* depends on training progress rather than on the gap"). A test whose predicted label under "no width effect at all" is the label it reports cannot support a width or capacity reading. The sealed reading concedes this ("It is not by itself evidence of a capacity effect", N5 RESULT §2 reading 3), and the chain repeats it (chain §7 item 6), but the label CAPACITY_BEYOND_MEMORIZATION and the claim as phrased carry the opposite message.
- **What the saved data say about the gap.** Pooled over N6's 150 points (5 arms × 6 draws × 5 readouts), p\* = 0.033 + 0.129 × gap has R² = 0.902; adding training and validation NLL gives 0.921 (recompute: `N6_pooled_ols_of_p_on_loss_state`). The gap is not the whole story, but it is most of the variance, because p\* rises with training within every arm (N6 RESULT §6b: panel p\* 0.0566 → 0.0979 at 384, and likewise in every arm) and so does the gap. What is left after the loss state is a width-by-rate residual (768 at 3e-4 sits 0.005–0.0075 below its loss-state prediction; recompute: `residual_mean_by_arm_E3`). "Not a function of the gap alone" is true; "capacity beyond memorization" does not follow.
- **The gap is partly a corpus offset.** Training text is WikiText-103 and validation text WikiText-2's validation split, so the gap at E0 is already −0.034 (N5 RESULT §3b; N5 PROTOCOL §6a). Matching on it matches on memorization plus a fixed offset; minor, since the offset is nearly equal across widths.
- **M is small.** −0.0066 [−0.0076, −0.0055] on p\* of 0.06–0.10 (N5 RESULT §2); reliably nonzero, and about a tenth of the quantity.

**Severity: MAJOR.** As stated, the claim does not survive; it survives narrowed.

**What would resolve it.** Wording: rename the label (for example GAP_ALONE_REJECTED) and quote the epoch-only fixture rate wherever the label appears; state the claim as "p\* at matched train–validation gap differs between 384 and 768, as it also would if p\* depended on training progress". Reanalysis (free): report, beside M, the M that the epoch-only fixture predicts from the measured readout curves, so the reader sees how much of −0.0066 progress alone accounts for. New experiment: a uniform-weight arm (as in claim 1), which would let p\* be defined against a progress-matched trained baseline instead of E0; or two rates per width with dense readouts so that gap and progress can be matched at once.

### Claim 4. "At rate 3e-4, p\* at width 512 is above 384 and 768 on fresh draws (INTERIOR_PEAK); at 1e-4 the fall from 512 to 768 reverses by epoch 3 (RATE_DEPENDENT)."

**Strongest counter-argument.** Both labels compare models after the same number of updates, and at the two rates the models are at different stages of training. Read at matched progress, the control reverses and the "rise" half of the peak nearly vanishes; what survives is a fall from 512 to 768 at both rates.

- **The arms are not at matched progress at E3.** Panel training NLL at E3: 512 at 1e-4 4.266 against 4.125 at 3e-4; 768 at 1e-4 4.115 against 4.107 at 3e-4 (N6 RESULT §6d; recompute_matched: `N6_panel_train_E3`). At 1e-4, 768 is as far along as at 3e-4 while 512 is well behind; the control compares a lagging 512 with a fully progressed 768. The rate effect on training NLL is +0.142 at 512 and +0.008 at 768 (N6 RESULT §6a).
- **RATE_DEPENDENT reverses under progress matching.** Reading 768 at 1e-4 along its own path at the training NLL that 512 at 1e-4 reaches at E3 (readouts E2.28–E2.49) gives D4 = +0.0132 [+0.0106, +0.0158], 6 of 6 positive; matched on the gap +0.0101 [+0.0076, +0.0126]; matched on validation NLL +0.0187 [+0.0135, +0.0239] (recompute_matched: `N6_contrasts_at_matched_progress.D4`). The sealed −0.0050 [−0.0090, −0.0010] (N6 RESULT §3) is what a fixed update budget shows when the wider model trains faster at that rate. The pooled loss-state adjustment agrees: the residual D4 at E3 is +0.004 to +0.007, excluding 0 under all three specifications (recompute: `N6_pooled_ols_of_p_on_loss_state.*.residual_contrasts_at_E3.D4`). The sealed reading's "so the fall seen at 3e-4 is rate-dependent" (N6 PROTOCOL §7) draws a causal conclusion from a fixed-update snapshot.
- **RATE_DEPENDENT is also fragile as computed.** Its upper limit is −0.0010; c03's value is −0.0003; leaving out c02 gives [−0.0102, +0.0003] and leaving out c05 gives [−0.0090, +0.0004], both spanning 0; leaving out c04 gives [−0.0080, −0.0000] (recompute: `N6_contrasts_at_E3.D4.leave_one_out`). With six draws the exact two-sided sign-test p is 0.031. The protocol admitted the control's power "is an assumption, not an estimate" (N6 PROTOCOL §10).
- **INTERIOR_PEAK's rise is mostly progress; its fall is not.** Matched on training NLL, D1 (512 − 384) is +0.0027 [+0.0005, +0.0049], 5 of 6; matched on the gap +0.0009 [−0.0006, +0.0024]; matched on validation +0.0077 [+0.0013, +0.0140] (recompute_matched: `...D1`), against the sealed +0.0192. D2 (512 − 768) is +0.0177, +0.0132 and +0.0261 under the three matchings, 6 of 6 each (`...D2`). The gap model predicts 0.013 of D1's 0.019 and 0.002 of D2's 0.016 (recompute_matched: `N6_gap_model_prediction_at_E3`). So the robust content is "768 is below 512 at matched progress, at both rates", and "384 and 512 differ mainly in how far they have trained by update 3,072".
- **The peak at E3 coincides with a peak in the gap.** At 3e-4 at E3, the gap at 512 exceeds the gap at 768 in 6 of 6 N6 draws, and in N5 the panel gap also peaks at 512 at E3 (0.5735 against 0.5640; N5 RESULT §3b), with Spearman 1.0 between panel p\* and panel gap over the eight widths at E3 and gap argmax 512 in 7 of 10 draws (recompute: `N5_p_vs_gap_over_8_widths.E3`; `N6_tally_512_above_768`). At E0.25–E1 the two order 512 and 768 oppositely in every draw (recompute: `N6_tally_512_above_768`), so p\* is not the gap; but the E3 peak is not independent evidence of a capacity peak either.
- **This is not Jane Street's peak.** Jane Street's p\* "often increases at small scales (particularly through tens of billions of parameters) and decreases at larger scales as we near the hundreds of billions of parameters regime" (blog read, item 4). A peak at 16.9 M parameters, in a ladder whose top is 34.7 M, sits inside Jane Street's rising segment, three to four orders of magnitude below their peak. The protocol says INTERIOR_PEAK "does not place the peak between rungs" (N6 PROTOCOL §7); `RESEARCH_GOAL.md` ("Update — 4 November 2026", "What this changes: **Finding (1) on natural text.**") files it under finding (1) regardless.

**Severity: MAJOR.** RATE_DEPENDENT does not survive as stated; INTERIOR_PEAK survives narrowed.

**What would resolve it.** Reanalysis (done above; should be reported beside the sealed labels with its interpolation caveat). Wording: "at 3,072 updates at 3e-4, p\* orders 384 < 768 < 512; at matched training progress 768 is below 512 at both rates and the 384–512 difference is within 0.003; at 1e-4 after 3,072 updates 768 is above 512, which progress matching reverses". New experiment to replace interpolation: readouts at E2.5 and E3.5, or the 1e-4 arms trained to the training NLL that 3e-4 reaches at E3 (about one more epoch at 512); one C1-sized run of three draws took 2.1 h (N6 RESULT §8), so about 2–3 h per added condition.

### Claim 5. "Per-width or common learning-rate choices can create or reverse p\* shapes across model sizes, so cross-size comparisons need the rate controlled."

**Strongest counter-argument.** The reversal cited is a progress artifact, the prescription is undefined, and the underlying fact is known.

- **What the rate changed was progress, not the ordering.** At matched training progress the ordering is 512 above 768 at both rates (claim 4). The unmatched rate effect on p\* at 512, −0.0161 [−0.0170, −0.0153] (N6 RESULT §6a), becomes +0.0030 [+0.0019, +0.0040] when 512 at 3e-4 is read at the training NLL that 512 at 1e-4 reaches (recompute: `N6_matched_progress.train.rate_effect_512_at_matched_interval`). A genuine width-by-rate interaction remains (768 at 3e-4 sits below its loss-state prediction; the rate effect at 768 is +0.0047 [+0.0015, +0.0079] with nearly equal training NLL, N6 RESULT §6a), but it is a few thousandths of p\*. The dip at 128 in N1 is descriptive, with width and rate changing together (N1 exploratory note §4), and NO_DIP_AT_ONE_RATE "cannot separate the rate itself from under-training at 64 and 96" (N3 PROTOCOL §8).
- **"Controlled" has no operational content in the project's own hands.** The per-width selection of N1 produced edge rates and Amendment 01's warning (N1 REPORT §4; §5 edge flags); the common rate of N3–N6 produced the confounds above. Neither is control. What the data call for is matching progress, or tuning each width to its optimum on a fine grid, or a width-aware parametrization.
- **The fact is known and the reference did it.** That the optimal learning rate of Adam-trained Transformers shifts with width under standard parametrization, so that a fixed-rate width ladder confounds width with distance from the optimum, is the premise of muP (Yang et al., "Tensor Programs V: Tuning Large Neural Networks via Zero-Shot Hyperparameter Transfer", NeurIPS 2021; a literature citation, not a project file; the project's own rule is to recheck primary literature before novelty claims, `AGENTS.md` §Scientific discipline). Jane Street tuned "hyperparameters to minimize loss against a validation set" and, for the largest models, fit "optimal hyperparameters as a function of scale" (blog read, item 5): the control the claim calls for is what the reference used.

**Severity: MINOR** as an observation (pre-registered in N6's control, real), **MAJOR** as a methodological contribution.

**What would resolve it.** Wording: "at a fixed update budget, the learning rate changes how far each width has trained and so the p\* ordering; p\* across widths should be compared at matched progress or at per-width tuned rates, as the reference did". New experiment, if the interaction is the contribution: a rate sweep (four rates) at 384, 512 and 768 on three draws at 3 epochs, reported as a surface over width, rate and progress. From C1's 2,290 s per draw for five arms with pre-training (N6 RESULT §8), 36 adaptations plus pre-training is about 4–5 h, two owner runs.

## 3. Issues not tied to one claim

**3.1 Scale and regime: nothing here is in the reference's regime.** Parameters 0.73–34.7 M (N5 PROTOCOL §3), against Jane Street's "tens of millions to hundreds of billions" (`docs/references/jane-street-blog-excerpts.md`); the whole ladder is at or below their smallest rung, where their p\* is small and rising (blog read, item 4). Data per draw: 2,097,152 unique tokens seen three times in N5/N6, 0.18–0.61 token-passes per parameter at 768–384; 196,608 unique tokens seen 32 times in N1–N4 (recompute: `tokens`). Pre-training is 4 epochs over 262,144 tokens from scratch, leaving E0 at about 5.5 nats per token on a 4,096-token vocabulary (N6 RESULT §6d; the uniform model is 8.3). A 35 M-parameter model on 2 M tokens is capacity-unconstrained in any ordinary sense; "capacity" in these studies means the ability to memorize the draw, and the project's own memorization figures show that is what moves (N4 RESULT §4b: gap 3.9 at 768 at E32). Jane Street warn that "the behavior of sequence weighting at small scales does not cleanly predict behavior at the largest scales" (blog read, item 8); that warning applies to this project's premise. The honest frame is the one in `RESEARCH_GOAL.md` §Scientific direction ("The current record does not establish ... an exact large-LM replication"), not the one in the claims table ("Reproduced in direction", `RESEARCH_GOAL.md`, "Claim classification", row "Jane Street finding (2)").

**3.2 p\* is progress-dependent by construction.** Gains are measured against E0, the pre-adaptation model (N1 PROTOCOL §4), so p\* is the weight-dependence of all learning since E0, generic and specific together. It rises monotonically with readout in every arm of every natural-text study (N6 RESULT §6b; N5 RESULT §3b; N4 RESULT §4a); the ratio of fit objectives J(p\*)/J(0) is 0.04–0.09 (recompute: `N6_fit_quality`, `N5_fit_quality`), so the w^p model fits well and the estimator is identified on text; what it estimates is a moving quantity. A fixed-epoch comparison of widths is a comparison of different stages. Jane Street's baseline for "loss reduction achieved from training" is not stated on the page (blog read, item 2); the estimator note allows any sequence-weight-invariant baseline (`docs/references/sequence-weighting-note.pdf`, p. 1). No natural-text study has a uniform-weight arm (N1 PROTOCOL §4: "There is no uniform reference arm"), so the weight-driven part of the gain is never isolated.

**3.3 The estimator's scale.** With weights on 0.01–10, p\* implies a ratio of expected gain between the heaviest and lightest sequences of 1000^p: 2.0 at p = 0.1, 2.2 at 0.117, 31.6 at 0.5, 10^24 at 8 (recompute: `implied_gain_ratio_1000_to_the_p`). Text values of 0.02–0.56 are interpretable; D2 = 0.016 is the difference between ratios of 2.24 and 2.01. The synthetic values up to 8 mean gains concentrated almost entirely in the heaviest sequences, a regime where the bounded search (`core.py` lines 42–52) decides the location. Jane Street print no p\* values on the page (blog read, item 3), so "meaningful relative to theirs" cannot be assessed from the repository.

**3.4 Fixed-effect factors that no interval covers.** Every draw of every natural-text study shares the pre-training pool of 2,048 sequences, the validation set of 512 sequences (65,536 tokens), the tokenizer and the pre-training recipe (N1 PROTOCOL §3–4; N6 PROTOCOL §2 "Shared by design"). After identical pre-training, 768 starts 0.06 nats worse than 384 on validation in every draw (E0 validation 5.5687 against 5.5084; recompute: `N6_E0`; N6 PROTOCOL §11 "E0"), a systematic recipe effect at the width the claims are about. Draw-to-draw spreads are tiny (sd of D1 0.0012, N6 ANALYSIS.json `contrasts.D1.interval.sd`), so "6/6" and p = 2e-7 measure sampling of articles, not robustness to the pipeline; any artifact of pool, tokenizer, initialization scale or pre-training length is a constant across draws and invisible to the t intervals.

**3.5 Pre-registration is internal, and public only after the fact.** Every protocol was sealed locally by hash and bound into the run terminals (N6 `peak_analysis.py` lines 50–59 check the subject in TERMINAL.json), and verified by the owner and the assistant. The only public registration, N6's pull request, was created about 22 minutes after Run C2 ended and before the analysis (N6 RESULT §7f; `NOTE_PUBLIC_REGISTRATION_EDITS.md`); it fixes the analysis in public, not the data collection, and the losses were on disk. No third-party timestamp exists for S or N1–N5. The "independent reviews" are briefs addressed to a model-based reviewer (`reports/natural-text-peak-analysis-approval-20261103-01/review-01/REVIEW_BRIEF.md` line 3: "You are an independent reviewer"); the project instructions are addressed to an AI assistant (`AGENTS.md` line 1). A referee would read "pre-registered" throughout as "pre-specified within one team" and discount it accordingly; the discipline is real, the external verifiability is not.

**3.6 Second attempts on the same seeds.** N1's Run B attempt 1 failed in d02 after d01 completed, with a GPU game running (N1 PROTOCOL §12); N3's attempt 1 failed in d06 after d01–d05 completed, charger unplugged and lid closed (N3 PROTOCOL §11). Both reruns used the same draws and seeds under an owner ruling that they were new attempts; that the completed draws' outputs were never opened rests on the owner's word, supported by the worker printing nothing and the analyses refusing attempt 1 (N3 PROTOCOL §11; N1 PROTOCOL §12). This is disclosed; a referee would still note that two of five natural-text runs are second attempts and that the "never opened" assurance is not independently checkable.

**3.7 Chain-level forking.** Each study's primary was chosen from the previous study's secondaries: N3 from N1's exploratory note (N3 PROTOCOL, "Disclosure"), N4 from N3's 27 unadjusted by-epoch intervals (N4 PROTOCOL, "Disclosure"; N3 RESULT §1c), N5 from N3's and N4's results (N5 PROTOCOL, "Disclosure"), N6 from N5's descriptive section (N6 PROTOCOL, "Disclosure"). Within each study the multiplicity is handled (Holm in N5; a fixed sequence in N4; a gated control in N6). Across the chain, the family of patterns examined before each confirmation is large and the confirmations test exactly the pattern that was seen; only N4 and N6 are fresh-data confirmations, and N3's MEMORIZATION_PREDICTIONS_MET and N5's three labels have no fresh replication. The chain discloses the flow (chain §8, "Seen-data flow") and does not adjust for it.

**3.8 Clipping.** Gradient clipping at norm 1 is active on 93–99% of updates in every natural-text study (N6 RESULT §6d: 0.931–0.975; N4 RESULT §4d: 0.971–0.993; N1 REPORT §6: 0.929–0.989) and 89–100% in S (S REPORT §6). The applied weighting is therefore not w: a batch whose heavy sequences raise the gradient norm is scaled down, and the degree differs by width and rate. The project notes "Clipping is therefore part of every weighting intervention here" (S REPORT §6) and has clipping diagnostics for the synthetic task only (`outputs/g1-clipping-diagnostic-v0129/`, listed in the git status); nothing quantifies the distortion on text.

**3.9 Width at fixed depth is the only size axis.** Depth 4 everywhere; 384 → 768 is 3.4× in parameters with the per-head dimension doubling (N3 PROTOCOL §2 parameter counts). Jane Street's ladders are "ladder rungs" of pretrained families (blog read, item 4). "Model size" claims rest on one architectural knob over a factor of 3.4 at the top.

**3.10 Near-null labels carried as findings.** N5's FALL CONTINUES_RISING has lower limit +0.0010 and 9 of 10 draws positive (N5 RESULT §2); its leave-one-draw-out lower limits go down to +0.0004 (recompute: `N5_FALL_E3.leave_one_out`). On N6's six draws the same statistic at the same rate and readout is +0.0014 [−0.0013, +0.0041], 4 of 6 positive (N6 RESULT §6b), i.e., not a rejection. The chain lists N5's CONTINUES_RISING as a prediction not met for Jane Street's pattern (chain §4) without noting that it is a near-null that did not re-reject on the next study's draws. N5's descriptive note already shows that FALL weights 512 at about a ninth of 768 and says little about the shape (`reports/natural-text-fewepoch-descriptive-20261103-01/DESCRIPTIVE.md` §1).

**3.11 Overclaiming, by sentence or label.**
- `RESEARCH_GOAL.md`, "Claim classification", row "Jane Street finding (2)": "**Reproduced in direction (pre-registered, 12 fresh synthetic corpora, 28 Oct)**". A sign match on a toy whose estimator saturates is not a reproduction.
- N5 label CAPACITY_BEYOND_MEMORIZATION (N5 PROTOCOL §5; N5 RESULT §2) and chain §7 item 6 "M contradicts the memorization-only account": the fixture gives the same label to an epoch-only curve (§2, claim 3).
- N6 reading "At 1e-4, 768 is above 512, so the fall seen at 3e-4 is rate-dependent" (N6 PROTOCOL §7) and chain §7 item 5 "At E3 that fall depends on the rate": a fixed-update snapshot read causally (§2, claim 4).
- N3 label MEMORIZATION_PREDICTIONS_MET (N3 RESULT §1a): the report's own §1c says the averages dilute the late epochs where the by-epoch secondaries read the opposite; the label survives only with that paragraph attached.
- Chain §2 table: N4 P1 and N4 P3 as two pre-registered confirmations (§2, claim 1).
- `RESEARCH_GOAL.md`, "Update — 4 November 2026", "What this changes: **Finding (1) on natural text.**": the ordering of three rungs at 17 M parameters is placed under Jane Street's finding (1), whose peak is at tens of billions.
- The sealed regime sentence "'closer to Jane Street's setting' means 3 epochs and no detected overfitting, nothing more" (N5 RESULT §2 reading 1) is accurate and should govern every summary; the claims as given in this brief drop it.

## 5. The three criticisms most likely to sink a submission, ranked

1. **The reference problem is not being tested.** Every model is at or below Jane Street's smallest rung; the datasets are 0.2–2 M unique tokens; "capacity" is the capacity to memorize a tiny draw; the overfit studies sit in a regime Jane Street regularized away, and the few-epoch studies share only the number 3 with their setting. Jane Street state that small-scale behaviour does not predict large-scale behaviour. A referee will say the paper's framing, title and claims table ("Reproduced in direction") are a category error, and will ask what the work shows about p\* that does not depend on that framing (§3.1; claims 1, 2, 4).
2. **Capacity is never separated from training progress and memorization, and the labels say it is.** p\* rises with progress in every arm; the gap explains 90% of pooled variance; M cannot distinguish an epoch-only curve; RATE_DEPENDENT reverses and INTERIOR_PEAK's rise disappears under progress matching; the late turn-down coincides with the widest model memorizing its draw. The sealed readings hedge correctly, but the label vocabulary (CAPACITY_BEYOND_MEMORIZATION, INTERIOR_PEAK, RATE_DEPENDENT, MEMORIZATION_PREDICTIONS_MET) and the claims built on it do not (claims 3 and 4; §3.2, §3.11).
3. **The learning-rate confound cuts the project's own results.** The study that shows the rate reorders p\* across widths (claim 5) is the reason a referee will not accept the fall at 768 measured at one unselected rate (claim 2) or the peak at a common rate (claim 4a); and the lesson itself is the muP premise, already applied by the reference's per-scale tuning (claim 5; §2).

A fourth, which a referee would raise alongside: pre-registration here is internal sealing checked by the same two actors, public for one study and only after its data existed, with two runs re-attempted on the same seeds (§3.5–3.6). It does not undermine the measured numbers; it limits how much the word "pre-registered" can carry.
