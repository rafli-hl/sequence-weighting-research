# Sequence-weight sensitivity under validation selection: a controlled synthetic case study

Working research note · 30 September 2026 · Evidence from completed Stages 2–10.

This draft organizes existing results. It introduces no experimental result, model selection, estimator replacement or new significance test. The accompanying [claim ledger](CLAIMS.md) identifies the final report versions, numerical keys, counting units and SHA256 hashes. The [focused primary-literature review](LITERATURE_REVIEW.md) records current research positioning; novelty and publication readiness remain unestablished.

## Abstract

Sequence-weight sensitivity is a potential obstacle to transferring data-mixture findings across model sizes. We study a fitted exponent, p*, that summarizes the relationship between assigned sequence weights and signed training-loss reduction, using a synthetic Transformer task with shared, group-specific and instance-specific answers. Across capacity-specific validation selection, matched optimizer/duration controls and pretraining interventions, the presence of a descriptive middle-capacity peak depends on the training policy and loss reference. A primary small adaptation benefit under one tuning panel fails the same useful-adaptation criterion at middle capacity in all four fresh panels; secondary policies do achieve useful held-out adaptation but do not show a uniformly surviving peak. Separate estimator studies distinguish availability, generating-exponent recovery, objective arithmetic and continuous-search agreement. Extreme synthetic cancellation exposes numerical ranking failures in our original evaluation, whereas all 179 defined retained model inputs pass the later objective-agreement checks. These results support a bounded methodological case study. They establish neither a general scaling explanation nor superiority over Jane Street’s study.

## 1. Motivation and scope

The project follows Jane Street’s question of how the effect of sequence weights changes with model capacity and training duration. Its proposed shared-versus-specific-pattern explanation motivates our controlled task. Our evidence concerns a small synthetic setting with three capacities. It cannot substitute for measurements across pretrained language-model families, broad scales and natural text. The single Pythia-70M size/seed in Stage 1 is a pipeline demonstration, not a scaling experiment. [Stage 1 report](../sequence-weighting-pilot/results-mechanism-v02/REPORT.md)

The central question here is whether an apparent exponent peak survives choices directed toward held-out performance. We then ask whether pretraining, the reference loss and estimator behavior limit its interpretation. The stages form an adaptive research sequence: each later protocol was frozen before its own results, while earlier findings motivated later questions. They are not nine independent confirmations of one hypothesis.

### Related work and claim boundary

The [Jane Street blog](https://blog.janestreet.com/a-study-of-sequence-weighting-at-scale/) supplies the motivating empirical pattern, and its [technical note](https://blog.janestreet.com/a-study-of-sequence-weighting-at-scale/sequence-weighting-note.pdf) supplies the cumulative estimator. Our finite [0,8] interval and absolute total-gain guard are implementation restrictions. Finite-sample stress failures do not refute the note’s asymptotic recovery statement under an exactly specified conditional power law.

Dependence of importance-weight effects on duration and regularization is established prior work. [Byrd and Lipton (2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/byrd19a.html) report diminishing influence with training and retained influence under some regularizers. [Xu, Ye and Ruan (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15209) connect weighting with optimization dynamics, implicit bias and generalization. Neither our Transformer experiment nor its gain exponent follows directly from an asymptotic classifier theorem. The review also identifies adjacent repetition/scaling studies whose outcomes and training-budget conventions differ from ours. Our candidate contribution is the particular controlled evidence chain and its failure accounting; the general observation that weighting depends on training is not a novelty claim. The ten-source review is focused, not an exhaustive priority search.

## 2. Task, estimator and experimental safeguards

The mixed task contains four shared-rule, four group-rule and four independent instance answers per sequence. Causal Transformers have widths 64/128/256 and 113,408/621,696/3,212,800 parameters. Random sequence weights are log-uniform; paired uniform-weight arms provide a training comparison, with undefined p*. Relevant arms share complete tokens and labels, initial checkpoints, weight assignments and batch orders, verified beyond example identifiers. Confirmation corpora are separate from tuning corpora. Model/weight seeds within a corpus are nested variation, not independent corpus replications.

For weights ordered consistently with the saved estimator, let G_i be the cumulative normalized signed gains and Q_i(p) the cumulative normalized powers w^p. The fitted quantity minimizes J(p)=mean_i[(G_i−Q_i(p))²] on our fixed interval [0,8]. Negative gains remain signed. Constant weights and the inherited total-gain guard produce undefined estimates; the implementation’s guard includes small positive totals at or below 1e-10. Training gains preserve the original operation order: subtract float32 losses, then promote the result to float64. Numerical audits use those saved rounded inputs.

The capacity contrast is K=p*_middle−max(p*_small,p*_large), with undefined inputs propagated. It is descriptive. Three capacities offer only one interior location and cannot identify movement between two interior peaks. Hyperparameter selection uses validation NLL and deterministic ties; p*, test loss, fit quality and visible peaks never select a model. R denotes random-arm validation selection; J denotes the mean across random and uniform arms. Selected durations can differ by capacity.

Useful adaptation, introduced in Stage 5, requires nonzero updates, positive initial-minus-selected test NLL in every corpus mean, and validation NLL no worse than the initial model in every corpus mean. Global utility requires this at all capacities. The separate scaling gate concerns decreasing test NLL across capacities. Earlier generalization gates are retained under their original definitions. A failure under one gate is not relabeled as a failure under another.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Selection changes the descriptive peak; duration matters

Stage 2 selected epoch 30/10/10 across small/middle/large capacities. Only 1/15 corpus/seed contrasts was positive; the mean across three corpus means was −0.007981, and the primary random-arm generalization criterion failed. At common epochs 3, 10 and 30, secondary trajectories instead had 15/15 positive contrasts. Thus the selected-policy result cannot identify regularization alone as the reason a peak disappears. [C01](CLAIMS.md)

Stage 3 crossed a fixed optimizer configuration F with the selected configuration J and common epoch30 with the selected 30/10/10 schedule. Mean K was −0.040267 for F/common30 and +0.073606 for J/common30; both selected-schedule comparisons had disappearance verdicts. The optimizer-by-duration interaction was −0.097161. These are paired contrasts on three new corpora, each with three nested model/weight seeds. J changes a configuration that includes learning rate and weight decay; the comparison does not isolate regularization. [C02](CLAIMS.md)

Baseline diagnostics also showed that shared-only pretraining left group and instance losses increasingly poor with capacity. Under J’s selected schedule, replacing the loss reference by an oracle shared answer and uniform group/instance predictions made all 27 capacity/corpus/seed estimates undefined. This arithmetic diagnostic motivates scrutiny of the initial reference; it does not establish the causal effect of a different pretrained model. [C03](CLAIMS.md)

### 3.2 Pretraining affects the peak and its interpretability

Stage 4 matched mixed pretraining tokens, cold initialization and batch order. M optimized shared-answer cross-entropy; U added uniform-target cross-entropy on group and instance positions. At fixed adaptation optimizer and epoch30, U−M changed mean K by +4.600252: U had a surviving descriptive peak and M did not. At epoch60, U’s peak disappeared. U reduced the intended initial group/instance losses while retaining shared accuracy. [C04](CLAIMS.md)

The striking epoch30 result comes with substantial qualifications. U’s middle-capacity mean objective was 2.600139 versus 0.000177 for M, and two of nine U fits reached p=8. The 15 main Stage 4 policy cells failed the random-arm generalization gate; six early secondary S/M cells passed it. Changing the pretraining objective may alter representations and learning dynamics as well as baseline loss. Swapping saved reference losses cannot separate these causal pathways. A larger K is therefore not evidence of a better fitted power-law mechanism or an improvement in useful learning. [C04](CLAIMS.md)

### 3.3 Primary adaptation utility fails fresh-panel follow-up; secondary utility succeeds

Stage 5 included the initial model as an explicit validation candidate and measured its test loss. For primary U/R/random, test NLL gains at widths 64/128/256 were 0.030936/0.001879/0. The largest model selected epoch0, yielding nine undefined p* values among 27 capacity/seed combinations and a failed global utility gate. The middle capacity passed at the corpus-mean level, although one of nine model/weight pairs worsened on test and three worsened on validation. [C05](CLAIMS.md)

Stage 6 repeated selection using four fresh tuning panels. Primary middle-capacity utility passed in 0/4 panels; the largest model chose epoch0 in 3/4. Global utility failed and K remained inconclusive through undefined values in all four panels. The four panels share three confirmation corpora and reuse selected checkpoints; 12 panel-by-corpus cells are not 12 independent corpora. Panel variation combines tuning data, pretraining and model/weight seeds. [C06](CLAIMS.md)

The negative result is specific to the primary U/R/random claim. M/R and M/J achieved global utility and the scaling gate in both arms in all four panels. Their random-arm peak verdicts remained mixed/inconclusive. U/J uniform also achieved global utility in three panels. These successful controls prevent a claim that useful adaptation is universally absent. They leave a more precise gap: a robust capacity-dependent peak supported by useful learning, a well-characterized starting model and adequate fit has not been established. [C07](CLAIMS.md)

### 3.4 Availability, recovery and numerical agreement answer different questions

Stage 7 retained all 8,320 estimator evaluations: 5,328 defined and 2,992 undefined. These came from 128 independent weight/noise blocks, with multiple paired conditions within each block. Noiseless in-range recovery above the guard had maximum error 8.52574e−12. At gain scale 1e−14 all 2,048 recovery evaluations were guarded, despite the underlying normalized profiles being scale-invariant mathematically. At cancellation parameter c=1e−12 all 256 evaluations were defined but conditional RMSE ranged from 3.84717 to 5.13481. Changing c changes signal-to-noise ratio along with cancellation; these results do not isolate cancellation as a cause. [C08–C09](CLAIMS.md)

Stage 8 revisited all 1,536 saved cancellation profiles. Among 1,024 comparable profiles, original binary64 objectives and naive anchored subtraction each matched the high-precision grid minimum in 987 cases; a factored evaluation matched all 1,024. All 37 original mismatches occurred in the 256 c=1e−12 profiles. This identifies a problem in our arithmetic implementation on extreme synthetic inputs. It does not show a flaw in Jane Street’s implementation, nor does grid fidelity establish generating-exponent recovery. The factored method changes both algebra and accumulation. [C10](CLAIMS.md)

Stage 9 checked relevance to the full prespecified model cohorts: Stage 4’s M/U fixed30 random arms and Stage 5–6’s M/U selected R/random policies. The 324 policy references map to 207 native checkpoints and 186 distinct weight/gain inputs. Seven inputs remained guarded. All three arithmetic methods matched the reference grid minimum for all 179 comparable inputs, with no tied minima or strict pair reversals. These are complete cohorts within the declared design, not all historical checkpoints. [C11](CLAIMS.md)

Stage 10 compared independent 80-digit derivative and 110-digit objective searches over [0,8]. All 179 defined inputs met between-search objective and parameter tolerances. Every original estimate met the separate objective tolerance; 175/179 met parameter tolerance 1e−6. The largest original parameter difference was 3.60684e−6. Objective tolerances were 1e−18·S between searches and 1e−12·S for original points, where S=max(1, reference-mesh objective span). Neither method includes the original point as a candidate. These finite meshes and local refinements provide bounded search agreement, not a global-optimality certificate. The prespecified weak-neighborhood flag was false in every comparable case. [C12](CLAIMS.md)

## 4. Interpretation, limits and research direction

The strongest supported contribution is a transparent set of boundary conditions for interpreting a sequence-weight exponent. Policy, initial reference, useful adaptation, statistical recovery and numerical fidelity require separate evidence. Passing a numerical check leaves failed utility gates unchanged. Likewise, a useful model need not exhibit a uniformly surviving middle-capacity peak.

Our principal unresolved alignment gap is useful learning tied to a controlled common-versus-specific-pattern intervention. M’s improvements coexist with initially poor group/instance predictions; U has a more neutral starting reference but its primary middle-capacity benefit failed follow-up. The current observations do not determine how much either setting measures learning shared structure, correcting overconfident initial predictions or memorizing examples. A future protocol should state a component-level learning target, compare against the model’s own initial losses, and hold other task characteristics fixed when changing pattern sharing. It should use fresh validation selection and confirmation, with all failures retained. Another numerical audit has lower priority absent a concrete new numerical failure.

The study has three capacities, few independent confirmation corpora, limited optimizer grids and frequent clipping. It neither explains Jane Street’s large-model rise-then-fall curve nor improves its scaling predictions or weighting algorithms. No statistical superiority, novelty, or publication guarantee follows. Stage 4 never measured its initial test losses: the 54 missing test-gain references in later cohorts stay null, while all 324 selected test measurements remain available. [C13](CLAIMS.md)

Protocols, source snapshots, raw losses, assignments, selection decisions, audits and versioned reports are retained locally. Documented analysis repairs preserve the originals. Stage 6 intermediate adapted models have recorded hashes and losses but not all intermediate binaries, limiting binary re-audit. Only equivalent scripts were executed, not the historical notebook. The appropriate current product is a bounded research note and a reviewed next protocol, with primary-literature comparison completed before any novelty claim.
