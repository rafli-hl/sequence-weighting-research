## Remaining argument gaps

1. Useful adaptation and a convincing capacity peak must be established together in a setting with adequate fit; current successful secondary utility does not settle the mechanism question.
2. A controlled common-versus-specific intervention must avoid changing task composition and starting competence in uncontrolled ways. M/U pretraining is informative but changes more than one scalar baseline.
3. A predictive mechanism would require a prespecified directional contrast that can fail. Post hoc relabeling of any curve as confirmation is insufficient.
4. More capacities are needed only if the claim actually concerns movement between interior peak locations; three capacities should not be sold as a scaling law.
5. Claims of novelty require primary-literature comparison. This ledger establishes local evidence support, not priority over previous work.
